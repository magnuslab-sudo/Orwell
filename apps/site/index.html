Use it on your phone, no app store

There is no app in the app stores. You put the browser app on your home screen. It gets its own icon, opens in its own window and you always get the latest version. It is the same app as orwell.pt/app.

Android in Chrome

  1. Open orwell.pt/app in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three dots, next to the address bar.
  3. Choose "Add to Home screen". In some versions of Chrome, this option is called "Install app".
  4. Confirm with "Install". The account you already had in Chrome is still there.

iPhone and iPad in Safari

  1. Open orwell.pt/app in Safari. In other browsers the option may not exist.
  2. Tap Share, the square with the arrow pointing up. On iOS 26 it can be inside the three dots button.
  3. Scroll down the list and choose "Add to Home Screen". If you see the option to open it as a web app, leave it on.
  4. Tap "Add".
  5. Create or import your account inside the new icon. iOS keeps the icon's data apart from Safari's data, and an account created in Safari does not show up there.

Before you install

No notifications while the app is closed. To reach your phone at that point, they would have to go through Apple or Google. That is a privacy decision we have not made yet. While the app is open, alerts arrive as they do in the browser.

You add your phone to your computer account from the computer. On the computer, go to Settings, "Devices", "Add device". A code appears. On the phone, on the start screen, choose "Yes, pair this device" and scan the code with the camera, or paste the link. Both screens show six words. Continue only if they match. The phone sees conversations from that moment on and does not have the account key: adding or removing devices is always done on the computer. Do not import the computer's .enclave file on the phone to keep using it on both. One account open in two places breaks the channel encryption, for good.

On iPhone, iOS 15.4 or later. Earlier versions have no encrypted calls. With Lockdown Mode on, Orwell does not work, because that mode turns off the place where the account is stored, and the calls.

The design was born on the computer. It works on the phone, but some screens are still cramped. We are adapting them.

Want to know how?

Encryption built for groups

We use the open standard RFC 9420, built for large groups. When someone joins or leaves, the keys change. Whoever leaves can no longer read what comes next. Whoever joins cannot read the messages from before they joined. They get the channel's board, posts, rooms and roles.

Post-quantum by default

The key exchange combines the classic method with ML-KEM, which resists quantum computers. Anyone who stores your messages today to open them in the future with one of those computers cannot read anything.

A server that does not read

The server keeps the encrypted messages for 30 days and delivers them to whoever holds the key. It does not know what they say. A two-letter reply is the same size as a paragraph. Times are stored to the minute, and not to the millisecond.

The account is a key pair

We do not store passwords, so there is none to steal. The password you choose opens the account on your device and never leaves it. There is no email to match against other data, and no password reset that anyone can trick.

Calls pass through us encrypted

Voice, video and screen pass through a machine of ours that relays them. The encryption uses a key of the channel itself, and that machine has no key at all. Only people who could already read the channel's messages can join the call. Encrypted voice already works in Chrome, Firefox and Safari. A browser that cannot encrypt the call connects directly to the others, without going through our machine.

Several devices, one account

Each device you add to the account gets keys of its own. What must not happen is the same account file being opened on two devices at the same time. The channel keys stop matching and there is no way to repair them afterwards. That is why the app warns you before it lets you try.

Orwell was made and is run by users like you. No company is waiting for your data. You do not even need an email to sign in.
